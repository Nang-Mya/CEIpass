What Chiang Rai Pass collects about you, why, and the choices you have.

1. Who is responsible

Data controller and contact for privacy requests: [to confirm: organisation name, address, email].

2. Data we collect

  • Account: your email address and display name. If you register with an email address, your password is stored only as a one-way hash, never as readable text. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your name, email address and that provider's identifier for you.
  • Security: the number of failed sign-in attempts and any temporary lock, the one-time codes sent to your email to reset a password (stored hashed and valid for 10 minutes), and a session cookie that keeps you signed in.
  • Purchases: the pass you bought, its start and end dates, the quantity, the amount, and the payment status and method.
  • Pass use: which partner attraction and benefit you used and when, shown in your history.
  • QR code: a signed code issued for your pass. It can be saved on your own device so it works offline until you remove it or sign out.
  • Your privacy choices: each optional choice you make, the notice version it was made under, and the time.
  • On your device: your shopping cart is kept in your browser's local storage. The session cookie is needed to keep you signed in.

3. Why we use it

  • To create and protect your account and sign you in.
  • To sell and activate your pass, show its QR code, record which benefits you have used, and show your history.
  • To send you the emails needed to reset your password.
  • Only if you choose it: marketing emails (offers and news about Chiang Rai Pass) and usage analytics (to help improve the app). Both are optional. Your pass works the same whether or not you agree.

  • Our cloud hosting provider, which stores the data on our behalf.
  • Our email delivery provider, for password-reset emails.
  • Google or Apple, only if you choose to sign in with them.
  • Partner attractions see that a valid pass was used, not your account details.
